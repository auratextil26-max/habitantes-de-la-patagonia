THE PINK ICON OF PATAGONIA

A graceful silhouette that brings color to Patagonia's wetlands.

The Chilean flamingo inhabits lagoons, salt flats and wetlands throughout South America. Its pink plumage, long legs and distinctive curved bill make it one of Patagonia's most recognizable birds.

It feeds by filtering tiny organisms from water and mud. Its survival depends on healthy wetlands where it can feed, rest and breed.

01

Pink plumage

Its coloration comes from natural pigments found in its diet.

02

Specialized bill

Its curved bill filters food from shallow water and mud.

03

Social species

It usually gathers in large flocks across lagoons, salt flats and wetlands.

04

Long migrations

It can travel great distances in search of food and suitable habitat.

CONSERVATION

Protecting wetlands means protecting the future of the Chilean flamingo.

The degradation of lagoons, salt flats and feeding areas directly affects its populations. Conserving these ecosystems also protects countless other species that depend on water.
